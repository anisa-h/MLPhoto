No shame in stopping here, let's rebuild the goal in plain English before touching code at all.

## What is this whole function actually for

You have a photo file. That photo file has hidden information stored inside it, camera brand, date taken, that kind of thing. That hidden information is called EXIF data. Your goal, the whole point of `get_photo_info`, is: open the photo, pull out that hidden information, and show it to yourself in a readable way.

## Where you are in that process right now

1. You opened the photo. Done, that's `image = Image.open(path)`.
2. You pulled out the hidden information. Done, that's `meta_data = image._getexif()`. At this point, `meta_data` is a dictionary full of entries, but every entry's label is a meaningless number, not a word. Something like `{271: "Canon", 306: "2024:03:15"}`, except in reality it's not two entries, it can be dozens.
3. What's left, the part you're stuck on, is: go through that dictionary, and for every single entry, turn the meaningless number into an actual word, "Make" instead of `271`, and then show both the word and its value on screen.

## Why a loop, restated simply

Because there isn't one entry, there are many. You can't manually type "show me entry 271, now show me entry 306," you don't even know in advance how many entries a given photo will have. So you tell Python "for every entry in this dictionary, do the same thing," and it repeats that action automatically for each one, that repeating action is the loop.

## What "the same thing" is, each time

For one single entry, say `271` paired with `"Canon"`, the same thing means:

- Take the number `271`.
- Look up what word it stands for, "Make."
- Print something like `Make: Canon`.

That's it. That's the entire task inside the loop, nothing more.
